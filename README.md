# Adaptive Stroke Rehabilitation Exoskeleton

## Introduction
Stroke is one of the leading causes of long-term disability worldwide, with incidence rising annually, particularly in aging societies [1]. Following a stroke, patients frequently experience hemiparesis. This is a partial paralysis or muscle weakness on one side of the body. Upper-limb deficits continue at six months post-stroke in 30–66% of hemiplegic patients, making recovery of upper-limb function a central rehabilitation goal [2].

Traditional rehabilitation relies heavily on repetitive exercises guided by a therapist. While effective, this approach is limited by therapist fatigue, scheduling constraints, and a lack of objective real-time monitoring [3]. Furthermore, current assistive devices often apply static, pre-set resistance levels that fail to adapt to the patient's fluctuating muscle strength, a common symptom of neurological fatigue, resulting in training that is either too easy to drive recovery or too demanding to be completed safely.
